# Research Sales Accounts

Turn an organization name, domain, or legal entity into a concise sales brief that tells an OpenAction employee what
to say, ask, and do next.

Use any extra context supplied (country, known contact, deal stage, meeting goal, suspected need, notes), but do not
ask for it. Ask only when the entity is ambiguous in a way public sources cannot resolve.

## Rules

- Research the web for every account, preferring current primary sources and public registries over aggregators,
  snippets, or marketing databases. Cross-check consequential claims.
- Label claims as `Confirmed fact`, `Supported inference`, or `Unknown`, with a direct source, a date, and a
  high/medium/low confidence.
- Never invent revenue, budget, headcount, technology, stakeholders, or intent, and do not treat a brand, website,
  campaign, parliamentary group, foundation, association, or parent entity as the same organization without evidence.
- Research is read-only and for legitimate B2B preparation: do not contact anyone, subscribe, submit forms, update
  external systems, or bypass logins, paywalls, robots rules, or technical protections.
- Follow the workspace answer-language rule unless the user requests another language, and keep official names in
  their original language.

Read [research-method.md](references/research-method.md) before researching, and use
[report-template.md](references/report-template.md) for the answer.

## Workflow

1. **Resolve the entity**: canonical organization, country, official domain, legal form, and any relevant parent,
   chapter, campaign, foundation, or operating entity. If several candidates remain, keep their findings clearly
   separate.
2. **Build the evidence**: cover the categories of `research-method.md` as far as they are relevant and available.
   Separate filed figures from estimates and proxies, and confirmed technology from browser-visible hints; not seeing
   a system does not prove it is absent.
3. **Assess fit** from evidence, without a numeric score: contact and member management, communication and
   mobilization, website/CMS/forms/events/payments/automation, fragmented tools or data, decentralized teams and
   permissions, scale, sovereignty and compliance requirements, and plausible capacity, timing, and procurement. Rate
   fit `Strong`, `Plausible`, `Weak`, or `Unknown`, explain the two or three deciding factors, and name any
   disqualifier.
4. **Form up to three sales hypotheses**, each with the suspected problem or opportunity, evidence, confidence, what
   would disprove it, and one discovery question. They are possibilities to validate, not claims about the buyer.
5. **Identify one to three contacts**, ranked by how well they can open a sale for this account, not by seniority:
   ownership of the likely problem, influence on the relevant decisions, ability to sponsor or evaluate a platform,
   link to the buying signal, recent evidence they hold the role, and a legitimate professional route. Aim for
   complementary roles when possible: a champion, a technical evaluator, and an executive or budget sponsor. For each,
   give their title, buying role, why they fit, evidence, confidence, recommended angle, and best verified route
   (official work email, contact page, professional profile, network channel, or warm introduction). If no one can be
   verified, name the role or team to approach through the official channel. One strong contact beats three weak ones.
6. **Recommend one next action**: personalize an approach, prepare a discovery call, go through a partner or warm
   route, monitor a signal, nurture until a trigger, investigate a blocking unknown, or disqualify for now. When
   recommending outreach, draft a short opening message based on one relevant professional signal, without
   surveillance-like detail, flattery, false familiarity, pressure, or unsupported claims.
7. **Deliver**: the brief comes first so a seller grasps the account, fit, and next action in about 90 seconds, then
   the supporting evidence. Say `Not found in public sources` instead of padding, and give the appendix only the
   evidence that supports the brief, unless the user asks for a detailed dossier.

## Privacy

- Include a person only for a relevant public professional role, limited to their name, current role, relevant public
  work, verified professional route, and the public source of that data.
- Never collect personal phone numbers, private emails, home addresses, family details, or unrelated social activity,
  and never infer an email address from a domain pattern or rely only on enrichment databases.
- Never infer political opinions, union membership, religion, health, ethnicity, sexual orientation, or other
  sensitive attributes; an official role in an organization is reported as such, without extrapolation.
- Present outreach as a draft for human review and, when personal data is involved, remind the user to apply the
  prospect-information, lawful-basis, opt-out, and suppression procedures before use.

## Before answering

Check that the entity matches the request; sources are direct, current, and varied; facts, inferences, and unknowns
are visibly separated; financial and technology claims carry their caveats; fit maps to real OpenAction use cases;
each hypothesis has a discovery question; each named contact's role and route are backed by a recent public source;
the action follows from the evidence; and every link supports its claim.
