# Research method

Use these categories as a guide; not every one needs to be filled.

## Source hierarchy

1. The organization's own websites, reports, accounts, policies, procurement documents, press releases, job postings,
   and public statements.
2. Government, court, company, charity, association, grant, lobbying, transparency, and procurement registries.
3. Official partner, funder, network, conference, and supplier disclosures.
4. Reputable news and specialist publications with named reporting.
5. Technology detectors, professional profiles, directories, and aggregators, only as leads to corroborate.

Use search snippets only to find sources, not as citations. Prefer sources from the last 24 months for current
strategy and technology, and older primary sources for history.

## Entity and organization

- Official and trading names, country, legal form, registration identifiers.
- Official domains and relevant subdomains.
- Parent, subsidiary, foundation, chapter, campaign, caucus, or federation relationships.
- Mission, constituencies, programs, geography, and languages.
- Governance and structure relevant to purchasing or deployment.

Watch for separate entities sharing a brand, or several brands under one entity, and record contradictions instead of
picking the convenient answer.

## Scale and operating model

- Members, donors, supporters, subscribers, volunteers, staff, offices, chapters, affiliates, territories, websites,
  campaigns, and events.
- Centralized or federated governance, and reliance on staff or volunteers.
- Recurring campaigns, elections, congresses, fundraising drives, petitions, consultations, or renewals.
- Multilingual and multi-country operations.

Label self-reported numbers as such, with their period.

## Buying signals

Look for recent changes that can create a need:

- new leadership or reorganization;
- new campaign, program, geography, chapter network, or membership drive;
- website redesign, migration, rebrand, accessibility work, or domain change;
- hiring in CRM, data, digital, fundraising, communications, security, membership, organizing, or IT;
- procurement, grant, budget cycle, election, congress, launch, or contract renewal;
- data incident, deliverability or outage problem, compliance concern, or stated tool dissatisfaction;
- new integration, supplier, or partnership.

Give each signal's date and why it matters for OpenAction; ordinary activity is not urgency.

## Finances and purchasing capacity

From official registries and the organization's filings:

- income, expenditure, and when relevant assets, reserves, debt, results, or cash;
- grants, subsidies, donations, dues, campaign funding, and major contracts;
- procurement rules, open tenders, framework agreements, and thresholds;
- fiscal year and reporting period.

Always state currency, period, consolidation scope, and whether a figure is filed, self-reported, press-reported,
estimated, or a proxy. Never turn a grant, campaign spend, assets, group revenue, or fundraising target into a
software budget. Without accounts, list only concrete proxies and mark capacity `Unknown`.

## Digital estate

Look in public pages and policies for:

- CMS and hosting;
- CRM, membership, fundraising, donation, petition, event, volunteer, survey, and form tools;
- email, SMS, marketing automation, and transactional messaging;
- payment processors;
- analytics, consent, tag management, and advertising tools;
- logins, member areas, data portals, APIs, and integrations;
- separate regional or campaign sites and inconsistent tooling.

Evidence strength:

- **High:** vendor named by the organization, supplier contract, public configuration, product-specific hosted domain,
  or authoritative case study.
- **Medium:** several consistent technical traces or current integration evidence.
- **Low:** one detector, script, cookie, DNS hint, old job posting, or undated directory entry.

Client-side detection misses server-side systems and can show leftovers of unused tools: report `Observed`,
`Likely`, or `Possible`, never a detector's guess as a confirmed deployment.

## Stakeholders

Search official team and governance pages, recent reports, press releases, job announcements, speaker pages,
professional profiles, partner pages, and procurement documents for up to three relevant professional contacts:

- an executive or elected sponsor;
- the owner of communications, digital, organizing, membership, fundraising, data, IT, security, finance,
  procurement, or operations;
- users, technical evaluators, budget owners, and external advisers;
- partners offering a credible warm route.

Rank them by:

1. **Problem relevance:** does the role own the likely pain or initiative?
2. **Buying influence:** can they champion, evaluate, fund, or approve a platform?
3. **Signal relevance:** are they linked to the observed reason to act?
4. **Currentness:** is there recent evidence they still hold the role?
5. **Reachability:** is there an official route or warm path?

A digital, communications, membership, organizing, fundraising, or operations leader is often a better champion than
the chief executive. Do not infer authority from a title alone: present the buying role as an inference to validate.

Acceptable routes are an official work email published by the organization, an official contact or team page, a
current professional profile, or a specific public partner or network connection. Never infer email patterns, report
personal contact details, or rely only on people-search or enrichment databases. If nobody can be identified
reliably, give the target role or team and the official route rather than lowering the evidence bar.

## Alternatives and competition

Identify confirmed incumbents and plausible alternatives: point tools and manual processes, in-house or agency-built
stacks, incumbent suites or sector platforms, and doing nothing. Avoid unsupported criticism of competitors; explain
the trade-off that matters for the account (consolidation, autonomy, implementation, integration, sovereignty, cost,
migration risk, or organizational change).

## Evidence ledger

Track consequential claims (identity, scale, finances, tools, signals, stakeholder roles, and anything that justifies
fit or outreach):

| Claim | Status | Confidence | Source | Published/updated | Accessed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Concise claim | Confirmed fact / Supported inference / Unknown | High / Medium / Low | Direct URL | Date or unknown | Current date |
